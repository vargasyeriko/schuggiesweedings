Barn Dance, Shindig or Ceilidh – Whats the difference?

Barn dances, Shindig or Ceilidh – what’s the difference?

Barn dances, Shindigs and Ceilidhs (pronounced Kay-leas btw) are all variations of a gathering or party that takes place in barns, houses and village halls all over the world 🌍. Don’t forget of course Tipi’s, Function Rooms, Marquees, Castles, Boats and even London Zoo🤯!

They’re all about socialising, having fun along with the music and dancing!

The three different styles usually have a Caller leading the dancing. A Barn Dance has more American associations- Stetsons and chequered shirts and line dances whereas a Ceilidh is historically a village party where singing 🎶, poetry, comedy stories and dancing were celebrated by tradition. Over the years it has diluted to focus more on the music and dancing side whilst still retaining the spotlight on socialising and having fun. A ceilidh would go off into the wee small hours🎉. Shindig is an Irish☘️ term meaning lively party which focuses on drinking lots of alcohol outside in the summertime.

What’s a Caller?

A Caller is the person up front with the mic 🎤 who is directs the dancing. A lot of folks come to these types of dances after hearing how much fun they are, watching them on YouTube or after being at a wedding that had one as the evening entertainment. These dances are nowhere near so technical that you need to be taught how to do them so a Caller is there to get the dancers set up and guide you through the dances💛.

It’s all about being inclusive and social. If you have any experience of doing this type of dancing when you were in school- these are a lot more fun and a lot less stressful. All the dances are simple enough for beginners, and the pace is fast enough for those with more experience to enjoy themselves. The emphasis is on having fun and being inclusive!😍

What’s a Barn Dance?

A Barn Dance is usually a country-western 🤠style social dance that originated in the United States and is still hugely popular here in the UK. The term Barn Dance can mean a few different types of dances. A slower ceilidh, line dancing, square, or contra 🎸; these are often done as couples or in sets (groups) with partners.

There is even a Broom dance for those of us so inclined🧹.

A barn dance is a fun social event. It’s an informal gathering where you can do some dancing, talk to friends, and drink some drinks🍻. Sounds familiar right?!

What’s a Shindig?

A Shindig is a type of ☘️party that you can have outside in the summertime. It’s usually lively with lots of instruments and people dancing. It also lasts until late with plenty of drinking going on. If you are brave enough, have a read of the antics annually at the Castlemorton Common Shindig and Worcestershire’s Shindig Home to join in the fun.

What about a Ceilidh then?

Traditional Ceilidhs have their Celtic roots across Scotland & Ireland where folk would come together to sing songs, tell jokes and stories as well as dancing✨. The focus of the night was for everyone to have fun, to be social and inclusive. Whilst ceilidhs have evolved to focus more on the dancing these days, that ethos of fun, socialising and being inclusive certainly hasn’t changed. When you evoke the image of a ceilidh, it should be folk dancing together without the stress of having to worry about getting it right🧡. Actually, mistakes are half the fun of this sort of ‘contact sport’. That’s where the inclusive side comes in, it doesn’t matter about ability, where you are from or who you are. It’s all about being comfortable within yourself and this makes ceilidh dancing accessible to anyone.

What do you wear?

If you are coming along in high heels, bring a change of shoes with you. Likewise layer up so you can strip off a bit, and still put them back on if it is frosty when you leave your venue.

Barn Dances usually involve cowboy/cowgirl attire, so Stetsons, boots👢 and chequered shirts are the order of the day- as well as something that represents a Country & Western style. No whips though, that’s a completely different type of party🤣.

For Shindigs & Ceilidhs, well anything goes. Tartan is always appreciated, and a kilt means you can show off skooshing across the floor and although it’s not a pre-requisite, who doesn’t like a swinging sporran, right?

One important thing to check for any sort of dance is whether the organiser has a theme for the evening. I’ve hosted all sorts, Cowboys & Aliens Barn Dances, Steam Punk, Pirates, Peaky Blinders, Outlander and Goth style ceilidhs💀. You can check out some of my reviews here📜.

Whether it’s a Barn Dance, Shindig or Ceilidh, what you wear doesn’t really matter as long as it’s comfortable to dance in. Depending on the occasion, such as a wedding or a posh Burns Supper then hot pants and ripped jeans might not be appropriate- however other than those restrictions anything goes.
