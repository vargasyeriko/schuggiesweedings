What do you give your wedding party as presents?

Giving a gift shows your appreciation of friends and family who have had significant roles to play in your big day.

These could be the Ushers, your Bridesmaids, Matron of Honour or Best Man (men). You might be generous and want to thank your Celebrant or Minister if the banter was that good😍.

There is something special about a bouquet or a bottle of good malt. These show you treasure the amount of effort the recipient put in to make your day go smoothly and are undoubtedly a pleasure to receive.

How does a Quaich work as a present?

What about a unique gift that, when used over the years, makes them smile, thinking back to your special day? What about a Quaich (kweix)?

What is a Quaich?

The Quaich is a traditional drinking bowl, and its name derives from Gaelic cuach, simply meaning ‘cup’. However, the Quaich is not used for just any drink. The Quaich is reserved for a good old dram of whisky or brandy!

It’s one of those extraordinary Scottish things, unique in its own right along with Irn Bru, tartan and the holy deep-fried mars bar, of course!

There is a considerable amount of history that you can read online. Bonnie Prince Charlie and Sir Walter Scott are just some of the historical figures that have counted a Quaich amongst their prized possessions.

Why would a Quaich work?

Of course, you don’t need to be Scottish to enjoy giving or receiving them. That’s one of the delights that make Quaichs such a unique gift.

Whilst it may sit on the shelf for most of the year, being admired by visitors- you can imagine the story that answers, “Oh, what’s that?” Your story, the memory of your special day and how they were part of it will be some reply.

The owner will undoubtedly use it for special occasions themselves; birthday’s, St Andrews night, Hogmanay and Burns are most common- and obviously a toast on your anniversary will probably be on the list.

Quaichs come in all shapes and sizes. And all sorts of materials from pewter and ceramic through to glass and wood. The wooden ones are a delight to hold, even the most basic design sings of craftmanship.

Price-wise, they are not dissimilar to a bottle of good malt or a decent bouquet either.

Of course, it is not the value of the gift that counts; it’s the thought that goes into it.

Whilst a Quaich is undoubtedly Scottish, not every Scot owns one. It’s a gift that symbolises friendship and thought. It shows how valued they are and that feeling comes back every time the Quaich gets used.

Can you imagine giving your bridesmaids a Quaich each, and then they use those as toasts at their wedding. How cool a story does that make?

I own a few, and I wear one on my kilt belt alongside my drinking horn when I’m out galavanting. Some of mine have been treats to myself, Some are presents, and my most precious is a gift from the Nottingham Scottish Association for the time I spent as their Secretary. It holds a special place in my Highland heart.

I’ve loved giving Quaichs as thank you’s to those who have supported my events over the years. Particularly Burns Suppers where speeches Immortal Memory and the Toasts require a lot of work from those delivering them.

Where do you start looking for these beauts?

If you are lucky enough to live in Scotland, your local kilt shop will be able to help.

For those further afield, then the internet has a wealth of options. It’s worth shopping around. My go-to’s are the likes of MacIntyres of Edinburgh and Eight Yards.

Both have a great range and reasonable prices.

Finally, if you are struggling for an anniversary present, a wooden Quaich for a fifth, ninth ( if you are in the States) is pottery, so a ceramic Quiaich. What about a Pewter quaich for a tenth anniversary?

Whether the Quaich is a gift or a trophy, you’ve chosen a unique vessel that reflects an ancient heritage and your appreciation of the folk receiving it.
